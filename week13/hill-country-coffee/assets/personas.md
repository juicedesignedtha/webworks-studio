# Hill Country Coffee — Supplied Personas

These personas are project evidence. Use them when writing user-need statements and prioritizing site requirements.

Do not add demographic assumptions that are not supplied.

---

## Persona 1 — Maya: The Mobile Regular

### Context

Maya lives in the area and stops for coffee several times each month. She usually visits the website from her phone while she is already out.

### Goals

Maya wants to:

- confirm opening hours;
- quickly find the nearest café information;
- check whether a familiar drink or food option is available;
- get the information she needs without navigating through several pages.

### Behaviors

- Primarily uses a smartphone.
- Often visits with a specific task in mind.
- Scans rather than reads long blocks of text.
- May be using the site while walking, sitting in a car before driving, or making plans with someone else.

### Friction

Maya becomes frustrated when:

- hours are buried;
- navigation labels are vague;
- pages load with large amounts of promotional material before useful information;
- menu information is difficult to scan on a small screen.

### Accessibility / Usability Consideration

Fast scanning, clear hierarchy, touch-friendly controls, readable text, and predictable navigation are important in Maya's context.

## Persona 2 — Daniel: The Hill Country Visitor

### Context

Daniel is visiting Central Texas and searches online for local coffee shops while planning a day trip.

He has never visited Hill Country Coffee before.

### Goals

Daniel wants to:

- understand what kind of place Hill Country Coffee is;
- decide whether it fits his plans;
- find location information;
- understand the general menu/offering;
- feel confident that the information is current.

### Behaviors

- May arrive from a search engine rather than the Home page.
- Uses both phone and laptop while planning.
- Looks for recognizable information patterns such as Menu, Locations, About, and Hours.
- Uses photographs and concise descriptive copy to evaluate unfamiliar places.

### Friction

Daniel becomes frustrated when:

- a site assumes he already knows the business;
- location information lacks context;
- navigation uses clever but unclear labels;
- important information appears only inside images.

### Accessibility / Usability Consideration

Content should make sense regardless of entry page. Important information must be available as real text rather than embedded only in graphics.

## Persona 3 — Renee: The Deliberate Planner

### Context

Renee likes meeting friends at local cafés but prefers to review options before choosing a location.

She may spend more time reading than Maya or Daniel.

### Goals

Renee wants to:

- review menu categories;
- identify food and beverage choices;
- understand the atmosphere and values of the business;
- share useful information with a friend;
- plan a visit without needing to call the café for basic information.

### Behaviors

- Uses both desktop and mobile devices.
- Reads headings and supporting details.
- May zoom text or use increased browser text sizing.
- Values clear organization more than visual novelty.

### Friction

Renee becomes frustrated when:

- text is small or low contrast;
- layouts break when text is enlarged;
- menus depend on tiny PDF documents or images;
- links such as “click here” provide little context.

### Accessibility / Usability Consideration

Readable contrast, flexible layouts, meaningful headings, descriptive links, and text that remains usable when enlarged are particularly important.

# Using the Personas

Your discovery plan should not simply summarize these profiles.

Ask:

- What does each person need to accomplish?
- Where do their needs overlap?
- Which needs should influence navigation?
- Which needs affect responsive design?
- Which needs create accessibility requirements?
- Which tasks belong in the MVP?

Write one user-need statement for each persona using the format supplied in the Week 13 assignment.
